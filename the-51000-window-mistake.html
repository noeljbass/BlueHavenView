Documented 2026 California Court Case

The $51,000 Window Mistake That Proves Your Installer Matters.

In a 2026 California court case, a homeowner had 25 new windows and four exterior doors installed. After rain, the new windows leaked. A construction-defects expert testified the windows were not waterproofed with flashing and were not installed according to the manufacturer's instructions. The homeowner paid $51,000 to remove, reinstall, and waterproof the windows and doors; a California appellate court later affirmed a $55,000 judgment against the contractor defendants in that case. This did not happen in Tennessee — but the installation lesson travels.

What Actually Happened

What the court record says.

The opinion describes a leaking-window problem, the installation details identified in testimony, and the work required to correct it.

25 windows in the remodel

The residential remodel included 25 windows. The record also describes four exterior doors as part of the installation and later remediation work.

Leaks after the installation

After the new windows were installed, the homeowner testified they leaked when it rained. The homeowner later testified the leaks stopped after reinstallation.

Expert testimony on the installation method

A construction-defects expert testified that stains were consistent with water intrusion and that the windows were not waterproofed with flashing or installed according to the manufacturer's instructions. The opinion also states the installer used screws through aluminum frames rather than the designed straps, damaging frames, and that the correction required breaking exterior stucco, removing and reinstalling the units, and waterproofing them.

What $51,000 Actually Means

Keep the numbers in the right buckets.

The case includes more than one dollar amount. This page uses the remediation amount as the headline number and separates it from the affirmed judgment and the expert's larger proposal.

$51,000

What the record supports: amount the homeowner paid a handyman and another contractor to remove, reinstall, and waterproof 25 windows and four exterior doors; the appellate court held substantial evidence supported this remediation amount.

$55,000

What the record supports: total damages judgment against the contractor defendants, including $51,000 remediation plus $4,000 unfinished work; judgment affirmed.

$92,950.52

What the record supports: an expert proposed this full-replacement cost, but it was not awarded. It appears here only to prevent confusion with the supported $51,000 remediation amount.

This page does not say the contractor defendants actually paid or collected post-judgment funds. The public record supports affirmed liability and judgment, not post-judgment collection.

Installer Checklist

Use the same questions before any window or door project.

Before anyone installs your windows or doors, ask:

  1. “Which Tennessee license or credential applies to this project's county, scope, and price—and can I verify it through the state?”
  2. “What insurance covers your installation work, and can I see proof that the coverage is current?”
  3. “Will you follow the manufacturer's written installation instructions, including the required flashing and waterproofing details?”
  4. “What installation commitments and product warranties will be written into my agreement, including coverage and exclusions?”
  5. “Can I see recent local project photos or reviews, and who will be my day-to-day contact?”
Official Tennessee credential lookup: verify.tn.gov. Requirements vary by county, scope, and price.
